🧠 What are these visualizations?

We use multiple eXplainable AI (XAI) techniques to show where the model is focusing and why it predicted a PCB as Good or Bad:

  • Grad-CAM: Highlights deeper convolutional feature activations. Bright regions mean the network “looked” there when deciding defect presence.
  • Saliency: Shows raw sensitivity of the output to tiny pixel changes. High intensity means changing those pixels would most alter the prediction.
  • Integrated Gradients: Accumulates attribution from a blank (all–zero) baseline to the real image, reducing noise and giving more stable importance.
  • Hotspots: Bounding boxes over the most activated Grad-CAM zones (requires OpenCV) to visually isolate suspect areas.

How to read them: Focus on overlapping bright regions across Grad-CAM and Integrated Gradients. Consistent activation usually signals a true defect rather than random noise.

Confidence vs Probability: For a 'Bad' prediction, confidence equals the raw probability; for 'Good' we show (1 - probability) to express certainty in non-defect classification.

Need deeper insight? Use the Interactive Viewer to zoom, measure distances, or click a point to sample localized activation strength.

Tip: Large bright areas near solder joints or trace gaps often correspond to manufacturing defects like shorts, opens, or corrosion spots.
